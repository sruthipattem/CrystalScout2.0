Methods and scope

CrystalScout rebuilds the screening method of Sendek et al. (Energy Environ. Sci. 2017; Chem. Mater. 2019) as a re-runnable two-database pipeline, and replaces its weakest link, the 40-material training set, with OBELiX.

The pipeline

  1. Stage A, property filters. Materials Project entries that contain Li, have a band gap above 1 eV, sit on the convex hull and contain no transition metals. Every failed rule is logged per material.
  2. Shared feature extractor. Each crystal structure, whether from the Materials Project, OBELiX or an upload, goes through the same code: CIF parsing, symmetry expansion, periodic neighbour search, and the five descriptors below.
  3. Training labels from OBELiX. Measured room-temperature conductivity ≥ 10−4 S/cm marks a material superionic.
  4. Ranking model. Logistic regression, the base paper's model family, retrained on OBELiX and validated three ways. Random forest and gradient boosting run alongside for comparison.
  5. Explained shortlist. Every candidate gets an exact per-descriptor explanation, an applicability-domain check and, when available, the measured OBELiX conductivity of the same composition.

Descriptor definitions

The papers define the descriptors in words. These are CrystalScout's exact operational definitions, applied identically to every structure.

DescriptorDefinition used
LLB Li–Li bond numberExpected number of Li neighbours within 3.0 Å of each Li site, averaged over Li sites.
SBI sublattice bond ionicityMean Pauling ionicity 1 − exp(−Δχ²/4) of framework cation–anion bonds (first coordination shell: anions within 1.25× the shortest contact). Li binaries use Li–anion bonds.
AFC anion framework coordinationMean number of anion neighbours of each anion inside the anion sublattice, counted to 1.12× the mean of its four shortest anion–anion contacts (bcc gives 8, fcc and hcp give 12).
LASD Li–anion separation distanceMean Li–anion bond length over each Li site's first shell (within 1.25× its shortest Li–anion contact), in Å.
LLSD Li–Li separation distanceMean distance from each Li site to its nearest Li neighbour, in Å.

Anions: any of N, O, F, S, Cl, Se, Br, Te, I present; otherwise the most electronegative non-Li element (H in borohydrides, P in phosphides). Partially occupied sites contribute their occupancy to every count and average. Sites closer than 1.5 Å (1.6 Å for Li–Li) are alternative positions of one atom and are never counted as neighbours. Checked on ideal lattices: rock-salt LiCl gives AFC 12 and LASD = a/2; a bcc anion lattice gives AFC 8.

In scope

  • Re-runnable Stage A against the live Materials Project API, plus a bundled snapshot
  • Full OBELiX integration with explicit caveat flags
  • CIF-based extractor for all five descriptors, with periodic boundaries and partial occupancy
  • Logistic-regression ranking model with a leakage-free held-out split and grouped cross-validation
  • Interpretability: exact per-descriptor contributions, applicability domain, descriptor explorer
  • Model comparison (random forest, gradient boosting) and an external DFT-MD benchmark

Out of scope, by design

  • Oxidation-potential filter (> 4 V). Needs grand-potential phase diagrams against all competing phases; future work.
  • New DFT-MD simulations. The base paper's ground-truth step, beyond this phase's compute budget.
  • Synthesis and lab testing.
  • New ML architectures. Logistic regression is kept for a fair comparison with the base paper and for interpretability.

How to read a score

P(superionic) ranks materials for further study. It is not a measured or predicted conductivity, and rank 1 is not proof that a material is the best electrolyte. The right wording for a high-ranked material is "promising candidate"; DFT-MD and experiment remain the validation steps.
